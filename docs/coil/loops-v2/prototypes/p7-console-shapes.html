Three places the console could live

The console content is settled — blocking items, deferred blockers, loop state. Where it renders is not, and the three answers have genuinely different consequences. The third one is not a loop feature at all, which is the interesting part.

Shape A A tab on the thread, ahead of the transcript

Opening a loop lands on Console. The transcript is one click away. This is the literal reading of "at any time I open the chat there should be a page where I have a questionnaire ready".

Not the decision. The plan takes the console as an overlay on the thread route instead, so the transcript stays the default view — a declared divergence from the stated requirement, not an oversight (PLAN §3). The reason is #38's: an overlay has nothing to toggle back from. A second default view needs a sticky per-thread toggle that has to survive reload, agree across two windows, agree on mobile, and be discoverable when it is wrong; and choosing the view means owning the thread route's render decision, which is ChatView.tsx territory (an existing seam row at churn 83) rather than the delta-zero overlay row. What is given up is one interaction. Shape A stays priced here because it is the fallback if the night's tail turns out to be what sends people back to typing "are you still working on it?".

Cheapest and most direct. The cost is that answering and reading are mutually exclusive — and at 9am, half the time the first thing you want is to skim what it actually did before you decide anything.
Shape B A right-panel tab, beside the transcript

The console sits in the existing right panel next to Diff, Agents and Terminal. You read the transcript on the left and answer on the right, at the same time. The panel already has a badgeCount concept, so the unread count is free.

The best ergonomics, at a real price. Measured: the kind union and its list, the tab factory, the actions array and two switch cases — across two upstream-owned files the fork does not touch today. Churn measured over the 60 days before merge-base a4cc1367b, the ledger's own method: 14 upstream commits on RightPanelTabs.tsx, 7 on rightPanelStore.ts, and there is no extension point — registering through the tab switch is the only way in. Both switches render the tab chrome only; the body comes from rightPanelContent's kind ternary in ChatView.tsx — an existing seam row, and a hot one (churn 83, risk 18094) — so two rows holds only if the tab passes its content through children. (This also answers wayfinder issue #112, "what does registering a right-panel tab actually cost", as a side effect: two rows, on files churning 14 and 7, plus that constraint.)
Shape C A global inbox — not a loop feature at all

Every thread that wants something from you, loop or not, in one list. Loops become the heaviest contributor to an inbox rather than the reason it exists — and threads you started by hand get the same treatment.

The most useful thing on this page, and the least loop-specific. Pending approvals and questions already pile up on hand-run threads — this surfaces all of them. It is also the one shape that keeps working if loops are turned off entirely.
These are not exclusive, and the sequencing matters. A and C share their entire data layer — the same three sources, filtered differently — so building A first and C second is nearly free the second time. B is the one to defer: it costs the most, it is the only one that touches upstream files, and its advantage (read while answering) partly disappears once the console summarises what each check-in did. My order: A, then C, then B only if you find yourself flipping tabs.
