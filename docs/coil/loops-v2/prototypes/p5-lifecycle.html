Arming a loop, and every state it can be in

Two switches stand between a thread and an unattended night, and no thread is ever armed implicitly. Below that: the full state machine, written as the words the pill actually shows — because the vocabulary is the feature.

1 · Arming, from the composer

The decision to leave a thread running is made at the moment you walk away from it, so it belongs next to Send — not in Settings. The chip is off by default and shows the whole contract before you commit: how many check-ins, when it stops, and what will end it early.

2 · Every state, as the words the user actually sees

Three families, and the difference between them is the whole point: running, waiting (on you, or on something else), and stopped. A user must be able to tell "it finished", "it ran out of rope" and "it died" apart without reading a transcript.

Read the "spends nothing" tags together. Nine of the fifteen states cost no budget, and that is deliberate: almost everything that stops a loop from firing is a good reason, and a design that burned a check-in on each of them would exhaust a six-check-in night without ever restarting the thread once. The only two states that spend are the two that actually restart work.
